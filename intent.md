# Intent: BrewChat recipe-to-basket demo
Author: Maks. Status: draft.

## Problem
Homebrewers find recipes online (Brewer's Friend, forums, blogs) in inconsistent formats, then have to manually cross-reference every ingredient against what their supplier actually has in stock. When something is missing, they are stuck hunting for a substitute themselves. This friction between finding a recipe and having the ingredients in hand causes drop-off and lost sales for suppliers.

## Proposed outcome
A chat assistant that takes a pasted recipe, matches its ingredients against a real supplier's live catalog, suggests a substitution (reasoned from the assistant's own brewing knowledge, not a rule table) for anything unmatched or out of stock, and returns a priced, ready-to-use shopping list. The real success measure for this side project is getting the supplier to agree to integrate it on his site, not the shopping list itself.

## Affected users and systems
- Homebrewers (end users) who paste a recipe and receive a shopping list.
- HopCellar (anonymized name for this repo), the one supplier used for this demo, via his read-only inventory and catalog API (`/json/products/all`, `/json/products/id/{id}`).
- Potential future stakeholders if a monetization model is pursued (an affiliate or referral arrangement with the supplier).

## Constraints
- One supplier only for this demo, no multi-supplier comparison.
- The supplier's API is read-only (inventory and pricing), it has no cart or order-creation endpoint, so this version stops at a generated shopping list, not a real purchase.
- No integration agreement exists yet with the supplier, this is a personal favor and demo meant to earn a real pilot conversation.
- No user accounts, saved recipes, or order history, stateless per session.
- No recipe scaling or process adjustments (batch size, water chemistry), substitution only, not recipe engineering.
- Web chat only, no mobile app.
- The assistant must stay scoped to beer recipes and brewing topics and decline or redirect anything else.
- No hard deadline. Informal suggested phasing is 2-3 weeks to a live demo with the supplier.

## Open questions
- Does the supplier want to receive orders generated by the tool as a formatted list, an email, or something else?
- Are prices in DKK, and should totals shown to the user be VAT-inclusive or exclusive?
- What would a monetization model look like if the demo leads to a real pilot (affiliate commission, referral fee, or paid access to the tool)?
- Are there legal or data-use terms attached to using the supplier's API and catalog data for a public-facing demo?
- Does the working brewing-ingredient category filter match the supplier's own sense of what counts as an ingredient, especially edge cases like all-grain kits or cask/specialty beer?
